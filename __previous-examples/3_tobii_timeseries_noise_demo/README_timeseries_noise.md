# Time-Series Noise Classification Demo

## Experiment concept

This demo examines how visual attention changes when people classify the
short-term variability of time-series signals.

Two physically different signal domains are used:

1. **Machine vibration** measured by an accelerometer mounted on a rotating machine.
2. **Photovoltaic (PV) power production** measured over a short daytime interval.

Both domains use the same three response classes:

- **LOW**
- **MEDIUM**
- **HIGH**

The full experiment contains **18 stimuli**:

| Domain | Low | Medium | High | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Machine vibration | 3 | 3 | 3 | 9 |
| PV power | 3 | 3 | 3 | 9 |
| **Total** | **6** | **6** | **6** | **18** |

The recommended procedure is to run the two domains as separate blocks. This
allows the participant instructions to use a domain-specific question while
keeping the actual classification task identical.

---

## Research questions

### Machine-vibration block

> **How large are the irregular vibration fluctuations in the signal?**

Interpretation of the classes:

- **LOW** — regular machine operation with small irregular vibration.
- **MEDIUM** — stronger short-term vibration, for example under increased load.
- **HIGH** — strong irregular vibration with transient mechanical disturbances.

The demo intentionally avoids diagnosing a specific fault. The participant is
asked to judge the magnitude of irregular vibration, not to decide whether a
bearing, shaft, or other component has failed.

### PV-power block

> **How large are the short-term fluctuations in PV power production?**

Interpretation of the classes:

- **LOW** — mostly clear-sky production with only small short-term variation.
- **MEDIUM** — several moderate reductions caused by passing clouds.
- **HIGH** — frequent and deeper power changes caused by rapidly changing cloud cover.

The PV signal is not generated by adding arbitrary white noise. Variability is
modelled as smooth, temporally correlated cloud-induced attenuation.

# Experimental hypotheses

## H1 — Visual complexity increases fixation count

**Hypothesis**

As the level of short-term variability increases, participants will require
more visual sampling of the plot.

Expected direction:

```text
LOW < MEDIUM < HIGH
```

for the number of fixations on the time-series plot.

**Rationale**

A low-variability signal contains a more regular visual pattern. A high-variability
signal contains more local changes that may attract attention and require additional
visual comparisons across the time axis.

**Primary metric**

- fixation count

---

## H2 — Medium variability produces the longest decision process

**Hypothesis**

The **MEDIUM** class will tend to require the longest viewing or decision time.

Expected qualitative pattern:

```text
LOW    -> relatively easy
MEDIUM -> most ambiguous
HIGH   -> visually salient
```

**Rationale**

LOW and HIGH signals are designed to represent the extremes of the classification
scale. MEDIUM signals lie close to the conceptual boundary between the two and
should therefore require more evidence before a response is selected.

**Primary metrics**

- total fixation dwell time,
- gaze-recording duration before response.

This is an ambiguity hypothesis rather than a simple monotonic-noise hypothesis.

---

## H3 — High variability produces broader visual exploration

**Hypothesis**

HIGH-variability signals will produce a more spatially distributed scanpath along
the horizontal time axis than LOW-variability signals.

**Rationale**

When fluctuations occur repeatedly across the series, participants should inspect
more temporally separated parts of the signal instead of relying on a small section.

**Possible operationalisations**

- number of unique time-series bounding-box regions visited,
- horizontal fixation dispersion,
- proportion of the time axis covered by fixations,
- number of transitions between distant time windows.

Expected direction:

```text
LOW < MEDIUM < HIGH
```

for time-axis coverage.

---

## H4 — Attention distribution differs between physical domains

**Hypothesis**

The spatial pattern of gaze will differ between machine vibration and PV power,
even when the final response class is the same.

### Expected machine-vibration pattern

Machine vibration contains repeated oscillations and broadband irregularity.
Attention should therefore be relatively distributed across the complete trace.

### Expected PV pattern

PV variability is produced by localised cloud passages. Attention should be more
strongly concentrated around visible drops and recoveries in generated power.

**Primary comparison**

For the same LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH label, compare:

- fixation distribution across time,
- local dwell-time peaks,
- transitions between time windows.

This hypothesis demonstrates that the same abstract task — judging variability —
can involve different visual strategies depending on the physical process that
generated the data.

---

## H5 — Medium signals produce the lowest classification accuracy

**Hypothesis**

Classification errors and uncertainty responses will be most common for the
MEDIUM class.

Expected direction:

```text
accuracy(MEDIUM) < accuracy(LOW)
accuracy(MEDIUM) < accuracy(HIGH)
```

**Rationale**

The medium category is intentionally less perceptually distinct than the two
extreme categories.

This provides a behavioural measure that can be related to the eye-tracking
measures from H1-H4.

# Stimulus-generation logic

## Machine vibration

Each machine signal contains the same basic rotating-machine structure:

```text
fundamental oscillation
        +
second harmonic
        +
temporally correlated irregular vibration
        +
optional transient vibration bursts
```

The three classes differ in the magnitude and frequency of the irregular component.

This provides a plausible interpretation:

- LOW: regular operation,
- MEDIUM: stronger load-related vibration,
- HIGH: pronounced irregular vibration and transient disturbances.

The simulation does **not** assign a specific mechanical fault diagnosis.

## PV production

Each PV signal consists of:

```text
smooth clear-sky production
        x
cloud transmittance
        +
small measurement noise
```

Cloud passages are implemented as smooth attenuation events rather than independent
sample noise.

This creates a natural progression:

```text
LOW    -> nearly clear sky
MEDIUM -> several moderate cloud passages
HIGH   -> frequent and deeper cloud passages
```

# Data representation

Each CSV row is one stimulus.

The current time-series loader expects:

```text
first column = stimulus identifier
middle columns = numeric time-series samples
label column = response class
```

The generated files therefore have the structure:

```csv
id,s000,s001,s002,...,s127,class
machine_low_01,...,low
machine_medium_01,...,medium
machine_high_01,...,high
```

No separate string-valued `domain` column is inserted into the experiment CSV,
because non-label columns between the ID and label are interpreted as numeric
time-series values.

The stimulus ID preserves the domain:

```text
machine_low_01
pv_high_03
```

# Recommended tobii-pytracker configuration

For each block use the `time_series` modality and `sample` bounding boxes:

```yaml
dataset:
  time_series:
    label_column_name: class
    bbox_model: sample
    path: datasets/machine_vibration.csv
```

For the PV block, change only the dataset path:

```yaml
dataset:
  time_series:
    label_column_name: class
    bbox_model: sample
    path: datasets/pv_power.csv
```

Using `sample` is convenient for the demo because the saved bounding boxes include
the sample indices. During analysis, adjacent samples can be grouped into larger
time windows if a less granular AOI representation is desired.

---

# Important implementation note: plot scaling

The current `TimeSeriesDataset` determines the vertical minimum and maximum from
the currently displayed series. Therefore, a demo based only on changing the
absolute amplitude of white noise could be misleading: per-stimulus scaling may
make small and large fluctuations appear more similar.

The generator avoids relying only on amplitude:

- machine signals share a common oscillatory baseline and differ in irregularity,
  transient bursts, and broadband variation;
- PV signals share a common clear-sky trend and differ in the number, duration,
  and depth of smooth cloud-induced events.

As a result, the classes remain visually distinct primarily because of their
**temporal structure**, not only because of their absolute numeric range.

# Suggested analysis

For each trial calculate:

- participant response,
- classification accuracy,
- fixation count,
- total fixation dwell time,
- horizontal fixation dispersion,
- number of unique sample/window AOIs visited,
- percentage of the time axis visually visited,
- scanpath transitions,
- response uncertainty if an additional fallback response is enabled.

Then compare:

```text
noise level:
LOW vs MEDIUM vs HIGH

domain:
MACHINE vs PV

interaction:
noise level x domain
```

For a full scientific study, participant and stimulus should be modelled as random
effects rather than relying only on aggregated means.

## Plot generation

The accompanying `generate_data.py` script creates not only the CSV files but also
clean high-resolution PNG plots for every stimulus. The plots are saved in
`generated_dataset/plots/` and intentionally do not include titles, so they can be
used both for documentation and as clean visual assets.


---

## Plot rendering choices

The generated PNG plots use:

- no plot titles,
- labeled axes,
- a light gray grid,
- larger axis and tick fonts,
- fixed Y-axis limits within each domain.

Using fixed Y-axis limits makes the LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH distinction easier to compare
visually across stimuli from the same domain.
